# haEWAS Figure Reproduction

This repository contains the source data and code required to reproduce the main and supplementary figures for the heterogeneity-adjusted epigenome-wide association study (haEWAS).

The reproduction workflow is provided in:

```text
Reproduction_figures.ipynb
```

Each code-generated figure has a dedicated directory containing its source data and exported panels. Figure 1 and Supplementary Figure 5 are conceptual schematics created with BioRender and are not generated by the notebook.

## Reproducing the Figures

1. Clone or download the repository.
2. Open `Reproduction_figures.ipynb` in Jupyter Notebook, JupyterLab, VS Code, or another compatible environment.
3. Install the required Python packages.
4. Run the notebook in order or execute the section corresponding to the figure of interest.
5. Reproduced panels will be saved under the corresponding `Fig*/figures/` directory.

The notebook uses the supplied panel-level source data and does not require redistribution of the original cohort-level methylation data.

## Figure Reproduction Guide

### Figure 1

Figure 1 presents the conceptual haEWAS workflow, including methylation average-to-heterogeneity conversion with A2H, phenotype association analysis, and downstream applications. It was created manually with BioRender.

### Figure 2

Figure 2 summarizes EWAS and haEWAS discoveries across 46 phenotypes. The panels compare association landscapes, phenotype-level locus counts, and the relative contribution of haEWAS across trait categories.

### Figure 3

Figure 3 evaluates haEWAS in systemic lupus erythematosus. It includes the overlap and genomic distribution of significant loci, methylation risk score distributions, cohort-level effect-size comparisons, and external disease-status prediction across three independent cohorts.

### Figure 4

Figure 4 examines the biological and regulatory relevance of Braak stage-associated loci. It includes functional and genomic enrichment, cis-eQTM analyses, blood methylation patterns at loci annotated to *SLC22A23* and *CPNE1*, and blood-based classification of advanced Braak stage.

### Figure 5

Figure 5 evaluates haEWAS-derived signals across TCGA cancer types. It summarizes locus discovery, survival prediction using average methylation and methylation heterogeneity, and representative survival associations.

### Figure 6

Figure 6 evaluates cell-type-specific differential methylation in hepatocellular adenoma. It summarizes DMCT discovery across liver cell types, overlap between cell types, association landscapes, and functional enrichment.

### Supplementary Figure 1

Supplementary Figure 1 validates A2H-derived methylation heterogeneity estimates using independent samples with matched methylation array and WGBS data.

### Supplementary Figure 2

Supplementary Figure 2 compares the distributions and cross-cohort consistency of average methylation, CAMDA-derived heterogeneity, and CHALM-derived heterogeneity before and after logit transformation.

### Supplementary Figure 3

Supplementary Figure 3 summarizes the proportions and total numbers of EWAS-specific, common, and haEWAS-specific loci across the 46 phenotypes.

### Supplementary Figure 4

Supplementary Figure 4 evaluates statistical calibration and biological annotation in the SLE analysis. It includes genomic inflation factors, QQ plots, functional enrichment, and genomic annotation enrichment.

### Supplementary Figure 5

Supplementary Figure 5 illustrates the discovery, feature optimization, model fitting, and independent validation workflow for the SLE methylation risk scores. It was created manually with BioRender.

### Supplementary Figure 6

Supplementary Figure 6 summarizes Braak-stage locus discovery and statistical calibration, including overlap with EWAS findings, genome-wide association patterns, genomic inflation factors, and QQ plots.

### Supplementary Figure 7

Supplementary Figure 7 compares TCGA survival prediction using average methylation, methylation heterogeneity, and their integrated feature set.
